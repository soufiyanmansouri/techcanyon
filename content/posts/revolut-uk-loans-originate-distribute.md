Revolut is moving into unsecured personal loans in the United Kingdom — a product high-street banks have treated as core for decades. The more interesting part is the balance-sheet posture: UK leadership and the founder’s long-running thesis both point to originating credit, then shifting most of the exposure off Revolut’s own books.

Francesca Carlesi, CEO of Revolut UK, has framed loans as one step toward making Revolut the everyday primary bank for UK customers. In comments to Reuters, she described a plan to sell much of the credit risk through securitisation and keep the loan-to-deposit ratio near about 6% — far below the roughly 100% ratio common at a traditional deposit-funded lender.

## What is launching

The product is starting with a limited cohort before opening more widely across Revolut’s UK base of about 14 million customers over the coming weeks and months.

Reported product parameters include:

- Unsecured personal loans from £2,000 to £25,000 (about US$2,680 to US$33,500)
- Advertised rates from 6.9% to 29.3%
- A credit card expected next, with mortgages described as a later ambition
- A target loan-to-deposit ratio around 6%
- A phased rollout rather than an overnight full launch

On LinkedIn, Carlesi said Revolut wants “responsible borrowing on our customers’ terms,” available where people already manage their money, and called loans “the first step in building our lending ecosystem” in the UK.

## Primary-bank ambition, secondary credit risk

Carlesi’s stated goal — becoming effectively the primary bank of choice for everybody in the UK — puts Revolut in rhetorical competition with NatWest, Lloyds Banking Group, Barclays, HSBC, and Santander UK. Product breadth matters for that story: current accounts and cards get customers in the door; loans, credit cards, and eventually mortgages are how many households decide who is “their bank.”

Underwriting is still a gap for a relatively young lender. Without decades of its own repayment history, Revolut is reported to buy external data to support credit decisions. Carlesi has said the firm is confident in its analysis and has not published an overall UK lending volume target.

That combination — big distribution, cautious book size — is the product thesis in one line.

## Why Storonsky keeps the book light

Co-founder and CEO Nik Storonsky has argued for years that heavy lending turns a fintech into a bank for valuation purposes. In a Financial Times interview, he put the firm’s loan-to-deposit ratio near 6% versus close to 100% at a typical bank, and said Revolut does not plan exposure above roughly 10% to 20%. Loans that do sit on the balance sheet are meant to be sold on — whole loans or via securitisation — with the aim of near-zero residual credit risk for the operating company.

His caution has a personal backdrop: time at Lehman Brothers and Credit Suisse before Revolut, and a public preference for avoiding complex risk. The UK loan launch fits that philosophy rather than contradicting it. Revolut is lending to customers; it is trying not to become a classic hold-to-maturity consumer bank.

## The market test underneath the launch

Originate-to-distribute works when investors want the paper. If securitisation demand softens, pricing widens, or regulators tighten expectations around risk transfer, a “light” lender still has to choose among slowing originations, holding more loans, or accepting worse economics.

For operators watching the space, the useful questions are practical:

- How quickly does Revolut convert pilots into full UK availability?
- How much risk actually leaves the balance sheet after securitisation haircuts and retained tranches?
- Does a credit card — and later mortgages — force a heavier book even if personal loans stay light?
- Can external bureau and alternative data substitute for long internal repayment history at scale?

## Partners around the stack

Revolut’s wider stack still leans on familiar rails. Google Cloud runs core infrastructure; a multi-year expansion announced in September 2025 included bringing Google Gemini models into fraud detection as Revolut chased a path past 100 million customers. Mastercard has been a partner since Revolut’s 2015 launch and powers a large share of European cards under a global agreement. Visa has issued Revolut cards in Europe since mid-2017 and extended the relationship through Visa Direct for business cross-border payouts using a card number.

Lending does not replace those partnerships. It adds a credit factory on top of payments, deposits, and distribution.

## Bottom line

UK personal loans make Revolut look more like a full bank to customers. The company’s own language still insists it should look like a distribution-and-origination machine to investors. Whether that split holds will depend less on the marketing of “responsible borrowing” and more on whether capital markets keep buying the risk Revolut prefers not to keep.
